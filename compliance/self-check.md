## 1. Cold email to distributors (CASL)

### 1.1 The rule in plain English

| Rule | Section and short quote (primary, opened) | Meaning for MatchDesk |
|---|---|---|
| No commercial email without consent and the required content | CASL s. 6(1): "It is prohibited to send or cause or permit to be sent" | Every outreach email, follow-up and closing note is covered. "Cause or permit" means a referral partner or an outsourced sender emailing for MatchDesk counts as MatchDesk sending |
| Content of every message | CASL s. 6(2)(a)–(c); SOR/2012-36 s. 2(1): name, "mailing address", and a phone number, email or web address | Business name, postal address, one contact route and an unsubscribe in every message |
| Contact details must work for 60 days | CASL s. 6(3): "valid for a minimum of 60 days after the message has been sent" [source: SRC-13] | Do not close the mailbox, number or address within 60 days of the last send |
| Clear and prominent | SOR/2012-36 s. 3: set out "clearly and prominently"; unsubscribe "readily performed" | Footer in normal text, not grey 6-point type; one action to unsubscribe |
| Unsubscribe | CASL s. 11(1): "at no cost"; s. 11(2): valid 60 days; s. 11(3): "no later than 10 business days" [source: SRC-13] | Reply "stop" by email counts; honour it at once, in any case within 10 business days |
| Implied consent by publication | CASL s. 10(9)(b): "has conspicuously published, or has caused to be conspicuously published" | Only an address the firm or person put on its own website, directory entry or letterhead, with no "no solicitations" note, and only for a message relevant to the person's role |
| Implied consent by disclosure | CASL s. 10(9)(c): address disclosed to the sender, no refusal, message relevant to role | After a call, an email to the address the person gives is allowed |
| Existing business relationship | CASL s. 10(10)(a) and (e): purchase within two years; inquiry within six months [source: SRC-13] | A distributor who bought a back-test may be emailed for two years; one who asked a question, for six months |
| First message after a referral | SOR/2013-221 s. 4(1): message must disclose "the full name of the individual" who referred and say it is sent because of the referral | One email only, naming the referrer in full. A second email needs a reply or another basis |
| Telephone calls are outside s. 6 | CASL s. 6(8)(a): "interactive two-way voice communication between individuals" | A live call needs no CASL consent. A recorded voicemail drop is a "voice recording" and is also outside s. 6 (s. 6(8)(c)), but see section 2 |
| Who must prove consent | CASL s. 13: the sender "has the onus of proving it" | Keep the proof for every emailable row |
| Only messages sent or read through a computer in Canada | CASL s. 12(1) | All of this plan's messages are covered |
| Who is liable | CASL s. 31: an officer or director who "directed, authorized, assented to" is liable; s. 32: employer liable for employees and agents | If the business is incorporated, the founder is still personally exposed |
| Limitation | CASL s. 23(1): three years after the matter became known to the enforcer | Keep records accordingly (1.2 item 11) |
| Due diligence defence | CASL s. 33(1): not liable if "they exercised due diligence" | This checklist, the funnel log and the proof per row are the due-diligence record |
| Maximum penalty | CASL s. 20(4): CAD 1,000,000 for an individual, CAD 10,000,000 for any other person [source: SRC-13]; s. 20(3) lists factors, including ability to pay and purpose "to promote compliance" | A maximum, not a tariff. The factors favour a small sender with records and a quick fix |

Government guidance (ISED, opened): check that a published address was "not accompanied by an indication" refusing messages, and be "ready to provide proof of this consent".

Not read (blocked): the CRTC's own guidance. Secondary, not opened by the author: the lead's C1 note summarises a law-firm article on CRTC decision 2016-428 (Blackstone Learning): the CRTC treats conspicuous publication case by case, the sender must show where and when each address was found, and an address copied or sold by a third party on its own initiative does not count. This document follows that cautious reading.

There is **no general business-to-business exemption** for messages between strangers. SOR/2013-221 s. 3(a) exempts messages only between people in the same organization or between organizations that "have a relationship".

### 1.2 What the business must do

1. Build the list as Decision.md section 12.3 says: one row per firm, and for each address the page URL, the date seen, a screenshot or saved copy of the page, the exact text near the address, and whether the page says anything like "no solicitations".
2. Mark a row **emailable** only if all four hold: the firm or person published the address on its own page or on a directory entry it submitted; no refusal statement; the address is for accounts payable, finance, purchasing, the controller or a general business inbox (`info@`, `accounts@`); the first message is about checking supplier invoices. Everything else is **phone only**.
3. Never mark as emailable an address that was guessed from a pattern, found by a contact-finding or enrichment tool, bought, scraped, or read from LinkedIn.
4. Put the footer of 1.3 in every email, including follow-ups and the closing note.
5. Send from a mailbox that receives replies and that will stay open for at least 60 days after the last send.
6. Keep a suppression list (the "do not contact" date in the funnel log). Before every send, filter the send list against it. Apply a "stop" reply the same business day; never later than 10 business days.
7. Check the suppression list also before calls and before any referral partner's introduction.
8. For a warm introduction, the first email names the referrer in full and says it is sent because of the referral; record the referrer and their relationship to both sides in the log.
9. A referral partner (fractional controller, QuickBooks consultant) who introduces MatchDesk uses their own consent basis and their own footer; give them the suppression list and never give them a list of addresses to email.
10. Do not add new people to an email sequence after they have unsubscribed, changed jobs or bounced; do not "re-verify" them with a tool.
11. Keep the funnel log, the per-row proof and the suppression list for at least four years after the last message to that firm. CASL sets no retention period; s. 23(1) bars proceedings "later than three years" after the matter became known to the enforcer [source: SRC-13], which can be later than the send, so four years is the safer choice `[assumption]` (author proposal).
12. Volume stays as Decision.md section 12.4 plans (100 new emailable firms a week, three weeks a month). A higher volume is a change to that section and needs this checklist rechecked.

### 1.3 Ready-to-use text (template - adapt)

**Email footer (every message):**

> [Founder's full name], [business name as registered]
> [Street address, City, BC postal code] · [phone] · [email]
> I wrote to you at this address because [Firm] publishes it on [page or directory name] as its contact for [accounts / finance]. If you would rather not hear from me, reply "stop" or use [unsubscribe link]. I will remove you straight away, and in any case within 10 business days.

For follow-ups the second sentence may be shortened to: "If you would rather not hear from me, reply "stop" or use [unsubscribe link]; I will remove you within 10 business days."

**Opening line of a referral email:**

> [Referrer's full name] of [referrer's firm] suggested I contact you, and I am writing because of that referral.

**Reply to a "stop" request (allowed: it is not commercial):**

> Done. You will not hear from me again. [Name]

### 1.4 Red lines

- No email to a phone-only row. No email from a contact-finding tool, a purchased list or a pattern guess, whatever a verification tool says.
- No LinkedIn connection note, InMail or direct message to open a sale (it is an electronic message to an electronic account; Decision.md section 12.4).
- No text message (SMS) to a prospect.
- No email without the footer; no unsubscribe that needs a login, a fee or more than one step.
- No message after a "stop", including "sorry to see you go" offers.
- No message sent through someone else's list or newsletter for MatchDesk unless they hold consent for it and their footer names MatchDesk.
- No misleading subject line or sender name; Competition Act s. 52 applies to electronic messages as well (see 7.1).

### 1.5 Residual risk: **medium**

The rules are clear and the checklist meets each one. What remains is interpretation: whether a given web page counts as "conspicuously published" and whether the message is "relevant" to the person's role is judged case by case, and the CRTC's guidance could not be read. The exposure is large in theory (s. 20(4)) and a complaint costs time even when the sender is right. The checklist's per-row proof and the low volume reduce it; it cannot be removed while cold email is used.

### 1.6 No-lawyer operating model `[P]` (owner decision)

1. **Warm first, cold second.** Lead with the ten warm introductions (Decision.md section 5) and referral partners, which rest on the referral exemption and on the partners' own relationships; these carry low risk.
2. **Phone first for anything doubtful.** A row that is not clearly emailable is a call, never an email (section 2 risk is lower).
3. **Email only after a reply or a call where possible.** Consent by disclosure (s. 10(9)(c)) is the strongest basis available without a relationship.
4. **Pause and fix, not argue.** On any complaint, stop all cold email the same day, review the rows, and only restart after section 10's steps.

### 1.7 When to get professional help anyway

- A complaint that mentions the CRTC, the Spam Reporting Centre, or "CASL"; any letter or notice from the CRTC (a notice to produce, a preservation demand under CASL s. 15 or 17, a notice of violation).
- A plan to buy or rent a list, use an outsourced sending service, send more than about 1,000 commercial emails a month, or email outside Canada.
- A referral partner who wants to send MatchDesk's messages from their list.

## 2. Telephone calls to distributors (telemarketing rules)

### 2.1 The rule in plain English

| Rule | Source | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| The National Do Not Call List rules do not cover calls to businesses | National DNCL Exemptions page (regulator page, opened): "The National DNCL Rules do not apply to telemarketing calls made to businesses." | Calls to a distributor's main line or a finance person's work line need no DNCL subscription or scrub |
| Statutory exemptions | Telecommunications Act s. 41.7(1): charities, existing business relationship, political callers, surveys, newspapers | The business-call exemption is not in s. 41.7; it is the CRTC's (as the DNCL page says) |
| Exempt callers still keep an internal list and identify themselves | DNCL Exemptions page: exempt telemarketers "must keep an internal do not call list"; a name goes on it within 14 days and stays three years; the caller must "identify the person or organization" at the start | Follow both, even though it is unclear whether the CRTC's Telemarketing Rules reach calls to businesses at all |
| Calling hours | **Not read** (CRTC rules page blocked). Secondary, a search-result summary of a CRTC page, not opened: 9:00 to 21:30 on weekdays, 10:00 to 18:00 on weekends, in the recipient's time zone | Calls only Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00 Pacific, which sits inside any reading |
| CASL does not apply to a live call | CASL s. 6(8)(a) | No email consent is needed to call |
| Review under way | Search results show a CRTC 2026 call for comments on the Unsolicited Telecommunications Rules (not opened) | Re-check the business-call exemption before any change in calling volume |

### 2.2 What the business must do

1. Call business lines only (main line, accounts-payable line, published work number). Never a personal mobile number unless the person gave it for this purpose.
2. Open every call with: your name, the business name, and why you are calling.
3. If asked not to be called again, add the firm and number to the "do not contact" column the same day (keep it three years) and do not call again.
4. Call Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00 Pacific, and not on BC statutory holidays.
5. No autodialler, no prerecorded message, no voicemail drop. A spoken voicemail by the founder is fine.
6. If the person asks for details by email, write down the address they give and their words in the log (CASL s. 10(9)(c)).

### 2.3 Template (template - adapt)

> "Hello, this is [name] from [business name] in Vancouver. I'm calling about checking supplier invoices against purchase orders and receiving. Is [Firm]'s accounts payable handled by you, or could you point me to the right person? ... If you'd rather I didn't call again, just say so and I'll take your number off my list."

### 2.4 Red lines

- No automated or prerecorded calls; no faxes; no calls to numbers marked "do not contact".
- No calls to residential numbers or to people at home.

### 2.5 Residual risk: **low**

The business-call exemption is stated by the regulator's DNCL page, and the checklist adds the internal-list and identification duties anyway. The calling-hours source is secondary, but the chosen hours are narrower than any reading. The CRTC review could change the exemption; the re-check in 2.1 covers it.

### 2.6 When to get professional help anyway

A complaint about a call, a CRTC letter, or a plan to use a call centre, a dialler, recorded messages or calls to consumers.

## 3. Privacy of prospect contact data (PIPEDA, BC PIPA)

### 3.1 The rule in plain English

| Rule | Section and short quote (primary, opened) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| BC PIPA does not treat business contact details as personal information | PIPA s. 1: "personal information" ... "does not include (a) contact information, or (b) work product information"; "contact information" covers name, title, business phone, address, email | For a BC business, a finance contact's name, title, work email and work phone in the prospect list are not personal information under PIPA |
| PIPEDA does not apply to business contact information used to reach someone about their work | PIPEDA s. 4.01: does not apply to business contact information collected "solely for the purpose of communicating" with the individual about their employment, business or profession | The prospect list used only to contact people about their job is outside PIPEDA's Part 1 |
| Which law applies to MatchDesk | PIPEDA s. 4(1)(a): applies to personal information collected "in the course of commercial activities"; PIPA s. 3(2)(c): PIPA does not apply where PIPEDA does | A BC business is under PIPA for what stays in BC; PIPEDA applies to information that crosses a provincial or national border (for example a US-hosted mail or extraction service). Both are met by the same checklist here |

### 3.2 What the business must do

1. Keep only business contact data in the prospect list: name, title, firm, work email, work phone, the page where it was published, and the funnel log columns. No home addresses, personal mobiles, birthdays or social profiles.
2. Use it only to contact the person about their work, never sell or share it.
3. Delete a row 12 months after the last contact unless the firm became a customer `[assumption]` (author proposal).
4. Name one person responsible for privacy (PIPA s. 4(3)): the founder.

### 3.3 Red lines

- No enrichment with personal data (personal email, home address, family details) and no profiling beyond the role.
- No sale, rental or sharing of the prospect list.

### 3.4 Residual risk: **low**

Both statutes carve business contact information out for exactly this use. The only gap is a row that holds more than business contact data, which the checklist forbids.

### 3.5 When to get professional help anyway

A request from an individual to see or delete what is held on them that cannot be met by simply deleting the row, or a complaint to a privacy commissioner.

## 4. Processing a distributor's invoices and records (service provider)

### 4.1 The rule in plain English

| Rule | Section and short quote (primary, opened) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| The distributor stays responsible for what it hands over | PIPEDA Schedule 1, 4.1.3: responsible "including information that has been transferred to a third party for processing"; must use "contractual or other means"; PIPA s. 4(2): responsible for information "not in the custody of the organization" | The distributor will need written terms from MatchDesk; MatchDesk should offer them first |
| A transfer for processing is a use, not a disclosure | OPC cross-border guidelines (opened): a transfer for processing "is a 'use' of the information; it is not a disclosure" | No extra consent from supplier staff named on invoices is needed, as long as MatchDesk uses the data only for the distributor's purpose |
| Same rules across borders, plus transparency | OPC cross-border guidelines: PIPEDA "does not distinguish between domestic and international transfers" | If any provider is outside Canada (hosting, email, extraction), say so in the letter |
| What is personal information in these documents | PIPA s. 1 excludes contact and work-product information | Supplier invoices, POs and receiving logs mostly hold firm data and business contact data. Receiver initials and signatures on delivery slips are the main personal information, and are work-product information under PIPA |
| Safeguards | PIPA s. 34: "reasonable security arrangements" | The controls in TRD section 8 and the B2 storage rules; in Phase 0, the no-copy model of 4.5 |
| Retention | PIPA s. 35(2): destroy once the purpose "is no longer being served" | The retention table of schema.md section 8 applies, including the 56-day backup tail |

The documents are commercially confidential to the distributor (its cost base). That is a contract matter, not a privacy-law one, and the letter in 4.3 covers it.

### 4.2 What the business must do

1. Before any real document is seen or copied (T-0.1.3, T-0.2.6, T-7.2.1), the controller signs the written permission of 4.3.
2. Use the documents only to produce the back-test and the pilot; never for marketing figures, other customers, model training or benchmarks.
3. Name every outside provider that will touch the documents in the letter (hosting, email, storage, extraction), with its country. Use extraction only with a provider whose terms exclude training on customer content (for AWS, the AI services opt-out policy noted in the lead's C1 check); Phase 0 hand back-tests use no extraction provider.
4. Delete on the schema.md section 8 schedule and confirm deletion to the controller in writing (email is enough).
5. Keep a one-line record per account: what was received, where it is held, when it was deleted.

### 4.3 Written permission to process documents (template - adapt)

> **Permission to process documents — [Distributor legal name] and [MatchDesk business name]**
>
> 1. **Purpose.** [Distributor] lets [MatchDesk] process its supplier invoices, credit notes, purchase orders, bills, vendor credits and receiving records for [test month] and earlier documents on purchase orders still open in that month ("the Records"), only to produce the back-test report and the pilot described in the back-test terms of [date].
> 2. **Role.** [MatchDesk] acts as [Distributor]'s service provider. [Distributor] decides what is processed and keeps control of the Records. [MatchDesk] uses them for nothing else: not for marketing, other customers, comparisons, or training any model.
> 3. **Where the Records are kept.** [Phase 0: in a folder that [Distributor] owns and shares with [MatchDesk]; nothing is copied to [MatchDesk]'s own devices or accounts.] [Application: on [hosting provider], [country]; files in [storage provider], [country].] Outside providers that may process the Records: [list with country]. Document reading by [extraction provider], [country], under terms that exclude training on the Records. [MatchDesk] will tell [Distributor] before adding or changing a provider.
> 4. **Who can see them.** Only [founder's name] [and named staff], each bound to keep them confidential.
> 5. **Security.** [MatchDesk] protects the Records with reasonable safeguards, including encryption in storage and in transit, access by named people only, and no Records in email bodies or chat.
> 6. **Incidents.** If [MatchDesk] learns that Records were lost, accessed or disclosed without authority, it tells [Distributor]'s contact the same business day, with what is known, and helps [Distributor] meet any duty it has to report or notify.
> 7. **Deletion.** [MatchDesk] deletes the Records [30] days after the pilot ends unless [Distributor] subscribes, or earlier on written request; encrypted backups expire within [56] days after that. [MatchDesk] confirms deletion by email.
> 8. **Confidentiality.** Prices, suppliers and findings are [Distributor]'s confidential information. [MatchDesk] may say that [Distributor] is a customer only with written permission.
> 9. **QuickBooks.** Where [Distributor] connects QuickBooks Online, [MatchDesk] reads only the records listed in the application, never writes to QuickBooks, stops reading on disconnect and deletes the copied QuickBooks data as described in the back-test terms.
> 10. **Ending.** [Distributor] may withdraw this permission by email at any time; processing stops and clause 7 applies.
>
> Signed for [Distributor]: ____________ Name, title, date ____________  Signed for [MatchDesk]: ____________ date ____________

### 4.4 Red lines

- No real document, name or price in the repository, in fixtures, in screenshots, in sales material or in logs (AGENTS.md section 4).
- No processing before the signed permission.
- No use of one distributor's prices or findings with another distributor, even anonymised (also an Intuit rule, section 6).
- No extraction provider that keeps or trains on documents.

### 4.5 No-lawyer operating model `[P]` (owner decision)

**"The distributor holds the data, MatchDesk works inside it."** For Phase 0 hand back-tests (T-0.2.6): the controller creates a folder in the distributor's own Google Drive, OneDrive or SharePoint, puts the QuickBooks reports (exported by the controller, so MatchDesk never holds a QuickBooks login), the invoice PDFs and the receiving log in it, and shares it with the founder. The working spreadsheet and report live in the same folder. At the end the controller removes the founder's access. MatchDesk then holds nothing, so most of 4.2's deletion and breach duties fall away. Once the application exists, the controller connects and uploads the records themselves (self-serve), under the same letter.

### 4.6 Residual risk: **low to medium**

Low for privacy: the personal information involved is small and largely excluded by PIPA s. 1. Medium for the commercial side: a leak of a distributor's supplier prices would harm it and end the relationship whatever the law says. The no-copy model of 4.5 lowers this most.

### 4.7 When to get professional help anyway

A distributor that sends its own data-processing agreement, asks for indemnities, insurance certificates or audit rights; a request for data residency guarantees the hosting cannot meet; any incident under 4.3 clause 6.

## 5. Breach of security safeguards: who must notify whom

This section answers the question left open in TRD.md section 7 ("Whether a legal breach-notification duty applies").

### 5.1 The rule in plain English

| Rule | Source (primary, opened) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| PIPEDA: report to the Commissioner and notify individuals | PIPEDA s. 10.1(1): a breach of safeguards "under its control" with "a real risk of significant harm" | The organization that controls the information reports. ## 8. Calling the report an "audit" (accounting-profession rules)

**Rule.** BC Chartered Professional Accountants Act s. 47(1)–(2): only authorised CPAs and firms may perform an "audit engagement" or an "assurance engagement" under CPA Canada standards or issue an opinion on financial-statement information. Section 46 keeps other people's right to "practice as an accountant". Sections 44 and 45 protect the CPA titles.

**What to do.** Call the product a back-test, check or exception report; never an audit, assurance, certification or opinion; never use "CPA", "accountant" or "auditor" for the founder unless true. Clause 6 of the back-test terms carries the disclaimer. Report footer (template - adapt): "Rule-based exception list prepared by [MatchDesk]. Not an audit, review or assurance engagement and not an opinion on financial statements."

**Red line.** No "audit-ready", "AP audit" or "certified" in sales material or the report.

**Residual risk: low.** The service is a deterministic comparison, not an engagement under CPA standards; the risk is only in wording. **Help anyway** if a buyer asks for the report to be relied on by its auditor or lender.
