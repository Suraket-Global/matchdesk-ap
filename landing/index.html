    How the back-test works

    You check one past month first. Nothing changes in your accounts and nobody needs your QuickBooks login.

    1. You send last month

      The supplier invoices (PDFs), and exports of your purchase orders, bills and receiving records, into a folder your company owns.

    2. We match every line

      Each invoice line is checked against its PO line and your receiving record, counting partial deliveries, earlier invoices and credit notes on the same PO.

    3. You get the report

      Only the lines that disagree, each with its dollar amount and the documents behind it, within 15 business days. Your controller approves it.

    A report you can put in front of a supplier

    Every flagged line names the invoice, the PO, the receiving entry and the rule it broke. Three numbers are kept apart so nobody overstates what was found.

    • FlaggedDiscrepancies the rules raised that no document, credit or receipt explains.
    • Confirmed by your controllerThe ones you agree are real overcharges, at the amount you confirm.
    • Credit recoveredWhat the supplier actually credited or refunded.

    Is it a fit? Better to know before you pay.

    It works when

    • You are a wholesale distributor (food, building supply, industrial) on QuickBooks Online Plus or Advanced.
    • Most purchases go through purchase orders.
    • Someone records the quantity that actually arrives: a warehouse log, receiver-signed slips, or item receipts entered at delivery.
    • You handle roughly 200 to 1,500 supplier invoices a month.

    It is not for you if

    • You use QuickBooks Desktop, Sage 50, or QuickBooks Online below Plus.
    • Few of your purchases have a PO.
    • You already run a three-way match tool.
    • Nobody records what arrives. Then we can still check prices and duplicates, but every line shows as "receipt absent", never as a pass.

    Questions controllers ask

    Our clerk already checks invoices. Why would this find anything?

    Maybe it won't, and then you get your fee back and a clear answer. The errors a person misses are the ones spread across documents: a second partial invoice on the same PO, a delivery recorded two weeks earlier, a credit that was promised and never issued.

    We don't record receiving in QuickBooks.

    That's fine. A warehouse log or receiver-signed packing slips work too; you upload the file and map its columns once. What does not count is the quantity QuickBooks shows as billed against the PO, or a supplier's slip with no receiver's mark.

    Do you need our QuickBooks login for the back-test?

    No. For the back-test you export the reports into a folder your company owns. For live checking afterwards, your controller connects QuickBooks with Intuit's own sign-in, and MatchDesk gets read-only access.

    How exactly is the refund decided?

    On the approved report, we add up the qualifying discrepancies: invoiced above received or ordered, price beyond your tolerance, duplicates, unmatched charges, and goods invoiced but not received where your log covers the order. If the total is under the fee, the fee is refunded. The rule is written down before we start.

    We use BILL. Doesn't it do this?

    BILL lists automatic three-way matching on its custom-priced Enterprise plan; its lower plans list two-way matching. If you are on Enterprise and it covers your receiving records, you may not need us.

    How much will we save?

    We don't know until we look at your month, and nobody should promise you a number before that. That is why the first step is a back-test with a refund.
